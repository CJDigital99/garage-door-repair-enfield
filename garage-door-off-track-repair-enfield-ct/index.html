A door that's come off its track is one of the most common — and most misunderstood — emergency calls we get. It usually happens after a minor collision (backing into a partially-open door on an icy Enfield morning is genuinely more common than you'd think), a broken cable letting one side of the door drop unevenly, or years of accumulated wear finally letting a roller slip free from its track entirely.

Do Not Force an Off-Track Door

If your door is crooked, hanging at a visible angle, or stuck partway with visible gaps between the rollers and the track, stop trying to open or close it immediately. Forcing it can bend the track further, damage the door panels, strain the opener beyond its limits, or in the worst case, cause the door to fall. What Causes Doors to Come Off Track in Enfield

  • Vehicle contact with the door while partially open: More common than it should be, especially on icy Enfield mornings when a driveway is slick and a car doesn't stop exactly where intended.
  • A broken cable or spring letting one side of the door drop unevenly: When one side of a door drops faster than the other, the rollers on that side are put under sideways stress the track isn't designed to handle, and they can pop free.
  • Bent or damaged track sections: Sometimes from age and general wear, sometimes from a stored item — a ladder, bike, or seasonal decorations — leaning against the track and gradually bending it out of alignment.
  • Worn rollers that have lost their ability to stay properly seated: Rollers wear down over years of use, and a sufficiently worn roller can eventually slip the track even under otherwise normal operation.

Our Repair Process

  • We assess the full extent of the problem first: An off-track door is sometimes a standalone issue and sometimes a symptom of an underlying cable, spring, or roller problem that needs to be addressed to prevent it from happening again.
  • We realign the door on undamaged track sections: Where the existing track is still straight and undamaged, we carefully guide the door back into proper alignment.
  • We replace any bent or damaged track: Where the track itself has been compromised, patching it isn't a durable fix — we replace the affected sections.
  • We swap worn rollers as needed: If worn rollers contributed to the problem, replacing them prevents a recurrence.
  • We rebalance the full system: After realignment, we test the door's balance and smooth operation across its complete range of motion on both sides.

What Off-Track Repair Typically Costs

Cost varies depending on the underlying cause — a straightforward realignment with no damaged parts is relatively affordable, while a repair that also requires track replacement or new rollers costs more. We'll assess the full situation and confirm pricing before starting any work.

Frequently Asked Off-Track Repair Questions

Is it safe to leave my door off-track overnight if I can't get a same-day repair?

We'd recommend not attempting to operate it at all in the meantime, and we can often prioritize an off-track call given the safety implications of a door in this condition.

Will insurance cover an off-track repair caused by a vehicle collision?

This depends on your specific policy — we're happy to provide documentation and an itemized invoice to support an insurance claim if that applies to your situation.

Can an off-track door fall completely?

In serious cases, yes, which is exactly why we recommend against continuing to operate a door showing these symptoms until it's been properly assessed.

Home/Services/Garage Door Cable Repair

If your garage door is hanging crooked, sagging noticeably on one side, or you can see a cable that's visibly snapped and dangling near the bottom bracket, it needs attention before you try to operate the door again. Garage door cables work in tandem with the springs under significant tension, and a failed cable can let a door drop unevenly, come off track entirely, or in some cases fall in a way that poses a real safety risk.

Signs Your Cable Needs Repair

  • Door hangs noticeably lower on one side: This is often the first visible sign of a cable problem, sometimes before the cable has fully failed.
  • A frayed or visibly damaged cable near the bottom roller bracket: Cables typically show wear before they snap completely — individual strands fraying, visible rust, or thinning at stress points.
  • A loud snapping sound followed by the door refusing to move evenly: Similar to a spring failure, but usually accompanied by the door tilting to one side rather than simply refusing to move at all.
  • The door reverses or jerks partway through opening: Sometimes a symptom of a cable that's slipped off its drum or is beginning to bind.

Common Causes We See in Enfield

Cable wear accelerates with age, rust, and a lack of routine lubrication — a common issue in older Thompsonville and Hazardville homes where the original cables have been in place for decades without ever being serviced. Road salt and moisture tracked into the garage over a Connecticut winter, season after season, accelerates corrosion on both the cable itself and the drum assembly it winds around, which is part of why we see a meaningful uptick in cable-related calls specifically in late winter and early spring, once accumulated corrosion finally reaches a failure point.

Our Repair Process

  • We assess both cables, not just the failed one: Like springs, cables typically wear at a similar rate on both sides, and replacing only the failed cable is a common cause of the door hanging crooked again within months.
  • We replace both cables as a pair when warranted: This isn't an automatic upsell — if one cable is significantly newer or shows meaningfully less wear, we'll explain that and recommend accordingly. But in most cases involving original hardware, pairing the replacement makes sense.
  • We re-tension the full system: Cables and springs work together, and after replacement we confirm the door is properly balanced across its full range of motion.
  • We run a complete operational test: Multiple full cycles, checking for smooth, even movement with no jerking, binding, or uneven tension on either side.

What Cable Repair Typically Costs

Cable repair is generally one of the more affordable garage door fixes we handle, and most jobs — including replacing both cables as a pair — are completed in under an hour once a technician is on-site. We'll confirm your specific price after inspection and before starting any work.

Why This Isn't a DIY Job

Like springs, garage door cables operate under real tension as part of a balanced system, and attempting to replace or adjust them without the proper tools and understanding of how the tension needs to be managed carries genuine injury risk. This is squarely in the category of repairs we'd recommend always leaving to a trained technician.

Frequently Asked Cable Repair Questions

Is a sagging door dangerous to operate?

We'd recommend not operating the door until it's been inspected — a compromised cable can fail further under continued use, and a door that's not moving evenly puts additional strain on the track and rollers.

Why do you recommend replacing both cables even if only one failed?

Cables generally wear at a similar rate on both sides of the door, and pairing the replacement helps ensure even, balanced operation going forward rather than a second failure a few months later.

How long does cable repair take?

Most jobs are completed in under an hour once a technician is on-site, and same-day service is the norm.

Can a bad cable damage other parts of my garage door system?

Yes — a failed cable letting the door hang unevenly puts extra strain on the track, rollers, and opener, which is part of why prompt attention matters even if the door still technically opens and closes.

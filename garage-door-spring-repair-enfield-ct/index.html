Home/Services/Garage Door Spring Repair & Replacement

If you heard a loud bang from the garage and now the door won't budge, or the opener is straining and groaning trying to lift it. You're almost certainly dealing with a broken spring. It's the single most common call we get from Enfield homeowners, and it's genuinely not a DIY fix: garage door springs are under enormous tension and have seriously injured people who tried to replace them without the right training, tools, and understanding of how the system works.

Torsion Springs vs. Extension Springs

Most Enfield homes built from the 1960s through the 1990s, a huge share of Thompsonville and Hazardville's housing stock, use torsion springs mounted horizontally on a shaft above the door opening. These wind and unwind to counterbalance the door's weight as it opens and closes. Older or lower-cost setups sometimes use extension springs instead, running along the horizontal tracks and stretching as the door closes. We carry both on the truck and will identify which one your door uses, assess why it failed, and explain the difference if you're curious.

Why Springs Fail in Enfield Specifically

Garage door springs are engineered and rated for a set number of open-close cycles, typically somewhere between 10,000 and 15,000 depending on the spring's quality and rating. In a New England climate, cold weather makes the steel more brittle and effectively accelerates the fatigue process, which is a big part of why so many Enfield springs seem to fail specifically in the dead of winter even though the underlying wear happened gradually across the entire year. If your spring is more than 7 to 10 years old (a reasonable rule of thumb for an average-use residential door), it's genuinely worth having it inspected before it fails rather than after, particularly heading into a Connecticut winter.

Signs Your Spring Is About to Fail

A spring rarely fails with zero warning, even though it often feels that way. Watch for a door that seems to be getting heavier to lift manually, an opener that's working noticeably harder or making more noise than it used to, visible gaps or separation in the spring coils, or a door that doesn't stay in place when partially open (a well-functioning, properly tensioned door should hold its position at any height without the opener's help).

Our Spring Replacement Process

  • We inspect both springs, even if only one has broken: This matters more than it might seem. The second spring is usually close behind on cycle count, and mismatched spring age between the two sides is a common cause of doors that hang crooked or wear unevenly even after a single-side repair.
  • We measure your door's exact weight and dimensions: Selecting the correct spring rating for your specific door isn't optional. The wrong spring is both a safety issue and a common cause of premature opener wear, since an improperly balanced door forces the opener motor to do work the spring system should be handling.
  • We replace and properly balance the door: After replacement, a correctly tensioned door should hold its position at any height without opener assistance. This is the test we use to confirm the job was done right, not just "does it open and close."
  • We test full-cycle operation before we leave: Multiple complete open-close cycles, checking for smooth, even, quiet operation on both sides.

Frequently Asked Spring Replacement Questions

Why did my spring break in the winter when it worked fine all year?

Cold weather makes steel more brittle, which accelerates the failure of a spring that's already been gradually wearing out through normal use. It's a seasonal pattern, not a coincidence.

Do I need to replace both springs, or just the broken one?

In most cases we recommend replacing both, since the second spring is usually close behind on wear even if it hasn't failed yet, and mismatched spring age is a common cause of uneven operation.

Is it dangerous to try opening the door manually with a broken spring?

Yes, the door becomes extremely heavy and difficult to control safely without the spring's counterbalance, and forcing it can also cause further damage to the door and track system.

How long does spring replacement typically take?

Most single or dual spring replacements are completed within an hour or two once the technician is on-site, and are typically finished same-day from the initial call.

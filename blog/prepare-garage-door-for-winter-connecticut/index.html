Home / Blog / How to Get Your Garage Door Winter-Ready

November is here, and if you haven't thought about your garage door since last spring, now's the time to fix that. Connecticut winters are no joke. We're talking sub-zero wind chills, freeze-thaw cycles that crack rubber seals overnight, and enough road salt spray in the air to corrode metal hardware in a few seasons. The good news: most winter garage door failures are preventable. Work through this checklist before the cold really sets in and you'll save yourself a cold Monday morning standing in a frozen driveway wondering why your car is trapped inside.

Quick Checklist

  1. Inspect garage door springs for age and wear
  2. Check and replace weather seals if cracked or compressed
  3. Lubricate all moving parts with white lithium grease
  4. Test safety sensors for alignment and response
  5. Inspect rollers for flat spots or wobble
  6. Check tracks for bends, gaps, and debris buildup
  7. Test opener performance and remote battery strength
  8. Know what to do if the door freezes to the ground

1. Check Your Springs Before the Temperature Drops

Garage door springs are under constant tension every day of the year, but cold weather makes them work significantly harder. Metal contracts as temperatures fall, which puts extra stress on springs that are already close to the end of their service life. Most torsion springs are rated for around 10,000 cycles, which works out to roughly 7 to 10 years of daily use for the average household. If yours are getting up there in age, winter is when they're most likely to give out.

What to look for: Does your door open unevenly, with one side higher than the other? Does it feel unusually heavy when you try to lift it by hand with the opener disconnected? Do you hear a loud bang from the garage that sounds like a gunshot when you use the opener in the morning? All three are signs your springs are struggling. A broken spring is a one-call fix, but it's not a DIY repair. The tension involved is genuinely dangerous without the right tools and experience. If you're seeing any of these signs, get your springs inspected before December.

2. Inspect Your Weather Seals (This Is Your Quickest Win)

Walk around your garage door and look at the rubber seals along the bottom, sides, and top. Cold temperatures and UV exposure break down rubber over time. It cracks, pulls away from the frame, and loses its ability to form a proper seal. Even a small gap at the bottom of a double door can let in a surprising amount of cold air and moisture.

Cracked seals also invite water in. When water pools under a door and freezes overnight, it can literally glue the door to the ground by morning. A new bottom seal typically costs $20 to $30 in materials and replacing it is one of the more manageable garage door tasks for a homeowner. If the side and top seals (the weatherstripping around the frame itself) are peeling or compressed flat, those should be replaced too. Take a look at our weather seal replacement service if yours are beyond a simple DIY fix.

3. Lubricate the Moving Parts

This is the single most effective thing you can do to extend your garage door's life and get through winter without problems. The key is using the right product. Skip WD-40 for this one. It's a degreaser and solvent, not a lubricant, and it'll attract dust and grime to your hardware faster than you'd expect. What you want is white lithium grease spray or a silicone-based lubricant.

Apply it to the rollers (hit the bearing, not the inside of the track), hinges, the spring coils (a light coat along the outside of a torsion spring), and the chain or drive screw on your opener if the manual calls for lubrication. One thing to skip: the tracks themselves. The rollers are designed to roll, not slide, and grease in the track creates buildup that causes more problems than it solves. A tube of white lithium grease costs about $6 and takes ten minutes. It's the cheapest preventive maintenance you can do. If you'd rather have a technician cover the full inspection in one visit, our maintenance and tune-up service handles all of this.

4. Test Your Safety Sensors

Your garage door's safety sensors sit about six inches off the ground on each side of the door opening. Cold weather can cause the sensor brackets to shift slightly, and when that happens, your door often refuses to close completely or reverses after moving only a few inches. It's a frustrating issue because it can appear and disappear as temperatures swing through the fall.

To test: start closing your door, then wave your hand through the sensor beam while it's moving. The door should reverse immediately. If there's a delay, or the door doesn't reverse at all, the sensors need attention. First try wiping the sensor lenses clean with a dry cloth. They're surprisingly sensitive to condensation and grime. If the problem persists, the bracket alignment or wiring may need adjusting. Our sensor repair service is a quick fix when the basic cleaning doesn't do it.

5. Look at Your Rollers

Worn rollers are the main culprit behind a grinding or rumbling garage door. Standard steel rollers typically last 5 to 7 years. Nylon rollers run quieter and handle cold temperatures better, since nylon stays more flexible than steel at low temperatures. If yours are steel and getting older, now's a good time to swap them out before winter.

Inspect each roller where it meets the track. If you can see flat spots or chips, or if the roller wobbles side to side when you push on it by hand, it needs replacing. Running the door on worn rollers puts extra strain on your opener motor and your springs, which shortens the life of both. See our roller replacement service for details on what's involved.

6. Check Your Garage Door Tracks

While you're looking at the rollers, run your eye along both vertical tracks from top to bottom. You're looking for bends, gaps, or sections where the track has pulled slightly away from the wall. Even a small bend in the track will cause problems. The door might bind, slow down on one side, or come off the track entirely.

Also look for buildup in the track channels. Road salt, dirt, and dried lubricant can accumulate over fall and create resistance. A quick wipe with a dry rag to clear debris is worthwhile before temperatures consistently drop below freezing. If you notice any visible bends or sections where the track has separated from the wall bracket, our track repair team can sort it before it becomes an emergency.

7. Check Your Opener's Performance and Batteries

Cold affects both the opener motor and the batteries in your remotes. When temperatures fall below 20°F, opener motors can strain noticeably, particularly older units with weaker drive mechanisms. If your opener is more than 10 to 12 years old and you've noticed it running more slowly, or if it hesitates before starting, winter will make that worse.

On the remote side: battery performance drops significantly in cold weather. If your remote's range has shortened, or it takes two or three presses to get a response, fresh batteries usually solve it. Keep a spare set in your car. If the remote works fine but the wall button doesn't respond, that's usually a wiring or logic board issue rather than a battery problem. Our opener repair service can diagnose it quickly, and if you've been thinking about upgrading to a newer unit with Wi-Fi monitoring and battery backup, our opener installation team can walk you through what's available.

8. What to Do If Your Door Freezes to the Ground

Even with a good bottom seal, an unusually wet night followed by a hard freeze can still cause the door to bond to the threshold. If that happens, the most important thing is to not use the opener to force it open. The motor will strain against the frozen seal and can damage the opener gears, strip the drive mechanism, or snap a cable.

Instead, try pouring warm (not boiling) water along the bottom seal, or use a heat gun on a low setting along the threshold. A plastic scraper or ice melt applied carefully to the floor just outside the door can work too. Once the door opens, inspect the bottom seal to see if it cracked or tore during the freeze. If anything breaks during the process, our emergency garage door repair service is available same-day across Enfield, Somers, Suffield, Windsor Locks, and the surrounding area. Also check whether a cable was damaged during the forced attempt, since snapped cables are a common outcome.

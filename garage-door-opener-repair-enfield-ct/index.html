Home/Services/Garage Door Opener Repair

An opener that hums but doesn't move the door, stops responding to the remote, or runs but doesn't actually lift the door is one of the most common repair calls we get across Enfield. Before you assume you need a full opener replacement, it's genuinely worth having a technician diagnose the actual issue — a significant share of "dead opener" calls turn out to be a much simpler, more affordable fix than homeowners initially expect.

Common Opener Problems We Fix

  • Opener hums but the door doesn't move: This is very often not actually an opener problem at all — it's frequently a symptom of a broken spring putting far more load on the motor than it's designed to handle, or occasionally a stripped internal gear.
  • Opener runs but the door doesn't fully open or close: Usually a limit switch or travel setting that's drifted out of adjustment, a straightforward fix that doesn't require any parts replacement in many cases.
  • Remote or wall button stopped working: This could be a dead remote battery, but it could also be the opener's internal logic board beginning to fail, which is worth a proper diagnosis rather than guessing.
  • Opener reverses immediately when closing: Almost always a safety sensor issue rather than a problem with the opener itself — see our sensor repair page for more detail.
  • Opener is completely unresponsive: Before assuming the worst, check the outlet and breaker — a surprisingly common cause after a Connecticut power flicker or storm, since openers plugged into an outlet on a tripped GFCI circuit simply won't respond to anything.

Our Diagnostic Process

  • We start with the basics: Power supply, breaker status, and whether the issue is actually the opener at all versus a spring, sensor, or mechanical problem elsewhere in the system — a large share of "opener" complaints trace back to something else entirely.
  • We test the opener's actual function: Motor operation, travel limits, force settings, and logic board response, to isolate exactly what's failing.
  • We explain what we find in plain language: Not a vague "it's broken" — a clear explanation of what's actually wrong, why, and what the realistic options are.

Repair or Replace?

Most opener issues on units under 8 to 10 years old are worth repairing — a new logic board, gear kit, or sensor is typically a fraction of full replacement cost, and we'll recommend repair when it genuinely makes financial sense. Openers older than that, or ones that have already failed more than once, are usually more cost-effective to replace outright given the accumulated risk of further failures, and we'll give you an honest recommendation either way rather than defaulting to whichever option costs more.

What Opener Repair Typically Costs

Simple fixes — sensor issues, limit switch adjustments, remote reprogramming — are generally quite affordable and often resolved within the same visit as the initial diagnosis. More involved repairs, like a logic board or gear kit replacement, cost more but are still typically well below the cost of full opener replacement. We'll confirm your specific price after diagnosis and before proceeding.

Frequently Asked Opener Repair Questions

My opener is humming but the door won't move — is this the opener or something else?

Very often this is actually a broken spring overloading the motor, not a true opener failure. We'll diagnose the actual cause before recommending a fix.

Should I repair or replace my opener?

Generally, repair makes sense for units under 8-10 years old having their first real issue; replacement often makes more sense for older units or ones that have failed multiple times. We'll give you an honest recommendation specific to your situation.

How long does opener repair typically take?

Most repairs are diagnosed and completed within the same visit, typically an hour or less once a technician is on-site.
